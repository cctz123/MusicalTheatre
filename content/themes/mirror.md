## The Stage as a Record of a Changing Nation

Broadway has never existed separately from American history. The country outside the theater has always found its way onto the stage. Sometimes it arrives directly. 1776 reconstructs the debate over American independence. Hamilton retells the founding of the nation. Parade confronts antisemitism and the Leo Frank case. Suffs dramatizes the struggle for women's voting rights. But Broadway records history in less obvious ways too. The immigrant families of Fiddler on the Roof and Ragtime reveal changing ideas about belonging. West Side Story turns ethnic conflict and urban inequality into tragedy. Hair captures a generation divided by Vietnam. Rent carries the memory of the AIDS crisis. Fun Home and Cabaret reveal changing representations of sexuality and identity. Company records evolving expectations surrounding marriage. A Chorus Line places ordinary working performers at the center of the stage. In the Heights asks what home means in an immigrant neighborhood undergoing economic change. Taken together, these musicals become more than entertainment. They become evidence of what America was thinking about itself.
