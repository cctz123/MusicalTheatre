## How Musicals Tell the Story of America

After moving chronologically through Broadway history and then examining how revivals allow old works to speak to new generations, this final gallery steps back. The question is no longer simply: How did Broadway change? It is: What can Broadway tell us about how America changed? Across more than a century, musicals have recorded the country's arguments about immigration and belonging, race and representation, war and patriotism, gender and sexuality, wealth and inequality, family and identity, and technology and social change. They do not record those histories neutrally. Musicals reflect the assumptions of the eras that created them—their blind spots as well as their breakthroughs. That is what makes Broadway a form of living history. A history textbook can tell us what happened. A musical can show us what a generation feared, celebrated, laughed at, romanticized, ignored, or imagined could be different. And when another generation revives that musical, the conversation begins again.
